## The mechanism gap (code-level)

The dual queue is explicitly labeled `TREATMENT: bounded-fair dual queue` with `neutralEvery = 5`: at most 5 consecutive guided expansions, then a forced attempt to take one candidate from the neutral FIFO lane. The scheduler therefore ACKNOWLEDGES that the frontier cannot cover every valuable state and that periodic unguided exploration must exist.

But cap retention ranks `goal 0 > guidedAdmitted 10 > everything else 30` and keeps the top `cap` by (rank, insertion). Whenever `guidedAdmitted` pending candidates alone reach the cap, EVERY rank-30 candidate is dropped - the FIFO lane is starved at retention time, and the scheduler's periodic neutral turn later has nothing left to take. The PR-5.25t first loss (checkpoint #8, `rankClass = 30`, `DROPPED_BY_CAP`) is exactly such a candidate.

## Primary mechanism (frozen)

```ini
FIFO_RESERVE = CEIL(pendingCandidateCap / (neutralEvery + 1))
FOR_CAP_1024_NEUTRAL_EVERY_5 = 171
```

Not a new tuning knob: it maps the scheduler's already-stated "roughly one FIFO opportunity per neutralEvery+1 expansions" onto the bounded pending pool.

Retention selection:

```text
1. goal candidates are always kept first
2. from the remaining pending, the OLDEST candidates (pendingSeq order -
   the neutralQueue's true FIFO contract) are kept, up to FIFO_RESERVE
3. remaining capacity fills guidedAdmitted-first by the existing
   (rank, insertion) semantics
4. a short lane yields its unused space to the other lane
5. total kept is still <= cap
```

```ini
FIFO_RESERVE_SEMANTICS = MATCH_NEUTRAL_QUEUE_ORDER
```

Deliberately NOT a `frontierGuided == false` reserve: the neutralQueue contains ALL children in creation order (guided ones included), so the FIFO turn can legitimately expand an old guided candidate; reserving the oldest pending matches that contract without inventing a third classification.

## Product-direction note (owner)

The dependency frontier stays GUIDANCE, not a completeness oracle. A bounded solver that wants to be reliable needs both a guided lane and a bounded-but-real unguided/FIFO exploration lane; demanding that the frontier know every correct-route action in advance walks back toward encoding the solution into the planner.

### Interpretation boundaries

- Single run. The reserve demonstrably perturbs the MT3 trajectory; whether the perturbed trajectory could still find MT3 within the frozen budget at full machine speed is UNKNOWN (the run stopped 27 expansions before the PR-5.25s goal-pop point).
- The mechanism contract micros all hold; what failed is the OUTCOME gate. The reservation semantics align with the neutralQueue FIFO contract and protect non-guided prefixes in principle - checkpoint #8's class of candidate is exactly what the reserve protects - but the displacement cost landed on the MT3 path in this run.
- Whether HEAD keeps or reverts the reserve is an owner decision (same discipline as `CHANGEFLOOR_REPAIR = KEEP` in PR-5.25q); this report does not self-authorize either direction.

The 32.3 vs 44.0 exp/s comparison was measured on PROVEN-DIFFERENT trajectories: different state combinations change closure sizes, legal-action counts, battle evaluations and signature work, so expansions/s cannot isolate machine speed, and the "27 expansions short on an identical trajectory" counterfactual does not stand. Re-running the 171 policy on a faster machine is NOT authorized - that would chase variance after an outcome-gate failure.

Design insight from the review (the round's main lesson): the scheduler's time-fairness ratio does NOT equal a pending-storage capacity ratio. The scheduler's real contract is only "when the next FIFO turn comes, at least one FIFO candidate must be available" - it does not require permanently protecting 1/6 of the stored pool, and because children per expansion vary highly, 1/6 of expansion opportunities has no mathematical reason to equal 1/6 of stored candidates. 171 was derived, not hand-tuned, but it is still a new heuristic mapping whose perturbation of the existing capability proved too large. PR-5.25v therefore shrinks the reserve concept to FIFO HEAD SURVIVAL: protect only the next live neutralQueue head the scheduler will actually consume.
