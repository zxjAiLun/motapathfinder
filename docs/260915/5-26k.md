## 1. Mechanism

`guidedAdmitted` is left exactly as it is: pure scheduler membership. What changes is only the retention class and one
survival guarantee.

```ini
RETENTION_CLASS_WITH_FLAG_ON =
  goal            0
  combatProgress 20
  otherwise      30
```

So a guided candidate no longer occupies a rank-10 slot by virtue of having been guided-admitted. Instead the cap trim
adds one protection, structurally identical to the neutral FIFO head protection PR-5.25v established: the guided
scheduler's **next live head** survives, because a scheduler cannot serve a candidate that retention already evicted.

```ini
TRIM_ORDER = goals -> base-rank pure fill -> protect guided head -> protect neutral FIFO head
```

Consequences that follow from the specified order, recorded rather than hidden:

- When the pure fill is full, each protection pays for itself by displacing the worst kept non-goal. The neutral head
  protection runs **last**, so in a cap-tight trim where both heads are live and different, the neutral head wins and
  the guided head is displaced back. That is counted as `guidedHeadDisplacedAfterProtection` instead of being reported
  as a save.
- The head is read by discarding only the entries the guided lane itself would skip (already expanded, missing,
  closed, stale retro-demoted) and then taking the top. No arbitrary removal from the middle of the heap, and no
  change to the lane's own pop path. Stale tops popped this way are counted.
- A guided candidate with combat progress falls back to rank 20, which is where the already-validated dynamic Pareto
  retention applies. That is the intended landing place for a case like checkpoint 29
  (`guidedAdmitted = true, combatProgress = true`): it loses the global rank-10 privilege but keeps investment-class
  retention.

This is a **survival invariant for the scheduler's next candidate**, not a storage reservation: no ratio, no quota, no
per-identity cap, no per-group cap, no new guided score.

Adjacent finding from reading the lane while implementing (recorded, not changed): the guided heap is pruned of
dropped ids by the trim itself, so a candidate that retention dropped cannot be served later by the guided lane. That
is why the stale-top discard sees only already-expanded / closed / retro-demoted entries in practice, and a probe at
cap 128 (3413 drops) observed zero "expanded after being dropped" events.

The mechanism does exactly what it was specified to do, and the prefix gets **worse**:

```ini
OFF first loss  = cp29  battle:MT2:6,6:brownWizard   rank 10  pureFillKept false
                  pendingRankCounts {10: 1039, 20: 0, 30: 0}   keptRankCounts {10: 1024}
ON  first loss  = cp14  changeFloor:MT2:6,0->MT1      rank 30  pureFillKept false
                  pendingRankCounts {10: 0, 20: 932, 30: 97}   keptRankCounts {10: 0, 20: 932, 30: 92}
```

Three measured facts, each independent of the others:

1. **The old failure is gone.** `rank10PendingAtEnd` is 0 in the ON arm; the all-rank-10 pool observed in OFF
   (`1039` pending, `1024` kept) cannot form. The rank-10 class is removed, not merely reduced.
2. **The new first loss is rank 30 with the pool dominated by rank 20** (`932` kept). So the decoupling did not leave
   the guided candidate unserved at rank 10; it converted the pressure into a rank-20-dominated pool topped by an
   ordinary neutral candidate.
3. **The guided pipeline itself collapsed.**

```ini
                     OFF       ON
 guidedExpansions      14791     2599     (x5.7 fewer)
 neutralExpansions      5209    17401     (ON is 87.0% neutral-lane)
 guidedAdmittedGenerated 19026    4544     (x4.2 fewer)
 liveGuidedPendingAtEnd   1021     116
 registrySize            51628    43583
```

That third row is the real finding. The rank-10 entitlement was not only a storage privilege that starved fresh
candidates - it was also bound up with keeping guided-admitted work **alive long enough to be generated and served at
all**. Once retention stops privileging guided membership, a guided candidate without combat progress is dropped in
its first retention pass (cp14: `guidedAdmitted = true, frontierGuided = true, combatProgress = false`, dropped at
rank 30), and the guided lane's admissions fall by more than 4x. Protecting only the head recovers 45 of 1142
opportunities (3.9%), far too little to replace the lost flow.
So under the current policy the coupling is **structurally load-bearing**:

```ini
GUIDED_RETENTION_PRIVILEGE_IS_LOAD_BEARING_FOR_GUIDED_THROUGHPUT = SUPPORTED_AT_FIXED_WORK_20000
EXACT_CAUSAL_CHAIN_FROM_EACH_DROP_TO_LATER_ADMISSION_LOSS     = NOT_INDIVIDUALLY_TRACED
RANK10_ENTITLEMENT_REMOVAL_ALONE = INSUFFICIENT
PREFIX_DEPTH_IS_NOT_MONOTONE_IN_RETENTION_EQUALITY = OBSERVED     (28 -> 13)
MEMORY_EFFECT_OF_THE_REMOVAL = RSS 1164.0 -> 927.4 MB             (secondary, and not a win that matters here)
```

Owner correction (applied): the sentence linking "this candidate was dropped" directly to "guided admissions fell"
is **too strong as a causal claim**. The reason is a counting one: `guidedAdmittedGenerated` is incremented at
frontier/skyline admission, when the candidate enters the heap, which happens **before** any cap trim. So a dropped
candidate cannot be identified with a missing admission. The chain the measurements do support is indirect:

```text
retention policy changed
  -> surviving candidates / expansion order changed
  -> successor generation trajectory changed
  -> how many later frontier-guided states were generated and admitted changed
  -> both guided expansions and guided admissions fell sharply
```

The fact remains that the guided lane lost 4x of its admissions and that the ON arm ran 87% of its expansions on the
neutral lane; what is retracted is only the claim that each dropped candidate explains a specific missing admission,
not the observation that the throughput collapse is real.

Read together with PR-5.26j, the two rounds bracket the problem: the **coupled** design saturates its rank-10 pool and
stops at 28; the **decoupled** design never saturates but loses its guided flow and stops at 13. Neither end of the
retention-class axis is the answer at fixed cap, which is why the owner's framing that guided admission-order
sensitivity plus pool pressure - rather than cp#29 specifically - is the real constraint continues to hold after this
round.

Non-claims:

- `guidedHeadRetention` is **not** promoted; default stays off. There is no result here that justifies turning it on.
- The gate was `PREFIX_ADVANCES_BEYOND_28 OR FOUND_MT4`, so this round is a **failed experiment by its own
  pre-declared gate**, and it must be reported as such rather than as a partial success.
- No MT4 run follows from a failed gate (`MT4_FROZEN_180S_RUN = NOT_RUN`).
- Attribution of the ON arm's cp14 drop to the mechanism is direct (the drop record says
  `rankClass = 30` and the pool composition is rank-20 dominated), but the *route* of the 28 -> 13 regression is not
  individually traced step by step; what is measured is the throughput collapse (fact 3), which is sufficient to
  explain why the prefix cannot reach 28 with 87% of the budget spent on the neutral lane.
- No tuning was attempted after the gate failed, per the round contract.

## 6. Honest scope and non-claims

```ini
STABLE_GUIDED_TIE_BREAK = DEFAULT_OFF / NO_MORE_DEVELOPMENT   (untouched this round)
RETRO_DEMOTION          = DEFAULT_OFF / CLOSED                (untouched this round)
CAP_OR_MEMORY_INCREASED = FALSE
FRONTIER_PATCH          = NOT_DONE
NEW_GUIDED_SCORE        = NOT_DONE
GUIDED_QUOTA_OR_RESERVE = NOT_DONE
PER_IDENTITY_OR_GROUP_CAP = NOT_DONE
```

- The mechanism is a retention change, so it **is** allowed to change the search trajectory; that is the point. What
  it must not do is change the scheduler, the frontier, the skyline, the neutral substitution or the cap, and it does
  not.
- `guidedHeadRetention` stays opt-in and default off in this round's commit.
- Phase 1 stub worlds are constructed, not sampled; they lock the contract, not the frequency.
- The 20k A/B runs at no wall limit on a loaded machine, so its wall figures are indicative only.
- Phase 1 of PR-5.26j remains the reason this direction was chosen: `ORDINARY_NEUTRAL = 0` over the tracked route, so
  the blocker was never "a class of steps the search cannot classify".
